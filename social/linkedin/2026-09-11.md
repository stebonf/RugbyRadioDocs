## Post

One of the most important UX decisions in Rugby Radio Live is also one of the least visible.

The person creating the commentary should not have to write a sentence during the match.

They should be able to record what happened with one hand, in a second, and get back to watching the game.

That constraint changes the product architecture.

The human provides a structured event.

The system keeps the match context.

The AI-Talker turns that context into a sentence with a specific style and language.

The spectators receive a live commentary that feels immediate, without asking the person on the sideline to become a full-time commentator.

This is a useful reminder when building AI products:

The best AI interaction is not always the one where the user talks directly to the model.

Sometimes the better experience is:

- make the human input small and unambiguous
- keep the domain state structured
- let the model handle the expressive layer
- keep the result traceable to the original event

AI is not replacing the person who sees the match.

It is removing the work that prevents that person from sharing it.

For me, that is the difference between adding AI to a product and designing a product around a real human constraint.

What is the smallest useful input your AI product could ask from a user?
