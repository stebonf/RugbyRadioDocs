Il flusso draft → verified → published rende la generazione più controllabile e mantiene l'interazione in partita rapida.

## Post

A useful AI feature does not always need to call a model at the moment the user taps.

In Rugby Radio Live, the commentator records a match event. The commentary text is already prepared for that event and the selected AI-Talker style.

We generate those messages ahead of time, review them, and publish the approved versions for the product to use.

That gives us a simple lifecycle:

Draft → verified → published.

It is a deliberate trade-off. We give up unlimited, improvised wording for each event in exchange for a faster and more predictable match-day interaction.

The review step also matters. Drafts are checked for text and language quality before they become available. In the documented workflow, automatic verification uses separate thresholds for text quality and language quality; the publishing job promotes a complete verified set.

This has changed how I think about AI in interactive products:

- Put generation where there is time to inspect the result.
- Keep the live action small and dependable.
- Make the boundary between draft and published content explicit.

The model is only one part of the feature. The workflow around its output determines whether the feature is ready to use.

Where do you place review in your AI products: before generation, between draft and release, or after users see the result?
