## Post

The most interesting part of Rugby Radio Live is not the AI-generated commentary.

It is the workflow around it.

A single tap during a rugby match can become:

- a live event
- a score update
- a piece of commentary
- an audio experience
- a match statistic
- a post-match article
- a social asset

That is the part I find most valuable as a software builder.

AI features are easy to demo when everything happens in one prompt.

Real products are different.

They need state.
They need retries.
They need clear ownership of data.
They need a way to understand what happened before, during and after the model generated something.

Rugby Radio Live started as a way to help people follow amateur rugby when they could not be at the field.

But technically, it has become a small laboratory for a bigger question:

How do you turn AI output into a reliable product workflow?

The answer, at least so far, is not "use a bigger model".

It is:

- model the domain carefully
- keep events as the source of truth
- make every generated artifact traceable
- separate creation from publication
- keep humans in control where judgment matters

That is where AI product work becomes interesting.

Not when the model writes something once.

When the system can use it responsibly, repeatedly and with context.
